## Notes

 - Not only *one* answer.
- It is ok if no final answer is reached, but the exploration process counts a lot.
- It should be a lighthouse: a reference point, a *lens* through which everything in the thesis is interpreted.
- Experimenting with the rules: iterating research questions and bending them without necessarily sticking to them from the get go.
- Generally, a pattern or a theme emerges from the [PMOMMs](PMOMMs.md)
- The research question ideally applies both to the project and the thesis: the former is investigated through practice, the latter is researched theoretically.

## First iteration of research questions draft

Here are the first research questions I am writing for the first time in my life—since honestly I did not really have one for [my bachelor’s thesis](Computer%20Sciences%20Are%20Social%20Sciences.md).

- How can decentralised, peer-to-peer, and encrypted communication protocols favour the development of autonomous and localised yet federated communities?
- What concrete communication technologies can already be adopted to prepare for a post-climate collapse world?
- How can practices of networking and collaboration benefit the re-population of rural areas?
- What role did the neoliberal progression of tech oligopolies play in the de-politicisation of technology?
- How can community-maintained infrastructure serve the needs of all the World’s population?
