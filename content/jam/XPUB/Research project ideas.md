These are original ideas for learning, research, and experimentation I had in mind *before* starting [XPUB](https://xpub.nl). As the academic year progressed, these ideas turned into [*Projects that May Or May not be Made*](PMOMMs.md).

## Everything is Rent

Given the rampant and prolonged [housing crisis in the Netherlands](https://www.theguardian.com/news/article/2024/may/06/netherlands-amsterdam-next-level-housing-crisis '‘Everything’s just … on hold’: the Netherlands’ next-level housing crisis'), and an unstable right-wing government unable to effectively address the issue, it becomes necessary to take the matter into our own hands and build cooperative, human, cultural solutions from below.

Be it squatting, cooperative housing, or any more specific structure, it’s evident that actually useful solutions occur mostly outside the institutional framework.

Inspired by <cite>[Rent](Rent.md)</cite> (1996), the absolute masterpiece by Jonathan Larson, it’s my intention to investigate the relationship between housing, arts, culture, and anarchist political practice.

What are the practices and devices non-profit cultural organizations take advantage of to survive the ongoing funding crisis for the third sector? Who are the individuals and collectives fighting the housing crisis in the front line in the Netherlands?

## Bridging hackerspaces

During the final assembly of [Reclaim The Tech](https://reclaimthetech.it) 2024, Geert Lovink and I discussed the significance and the role of the hacker movement. While Geert considers it to be <q>dead</q>, I believe that it simply fell asleep in its more blatant form. There are hacker cells all over the planet, shaping an invaluable infrastructure of participation and cooperation.

The most unique aspect I would love to investigate is the capacity of an hackerspace to have a deep and true relationship at the level of the neighbourhood, that is the most local and pragmatic level I can think of, while still being networked, connected, and coordinated at a global, universal level.

There are incredible humans, cyborgs, and robots *making* these marvellous networks happen, and I would love to investigate this phenomenon by interviewing them, reading more literature about the hacker movement, researching potential pathways for its future.

## Xperiencial Publishing

I have been spending countless hours exploring issue.xpub.nl and and project.xpub.nl, and I realised that my very ADHD definition of XPUB is of *Experiencial Publishing*. As I am the first victim of a fast-paced, tense, and anxiety-driven media world, I notice that I am unable to focus and truly commit to reading or *experiencing* something with my undivided attention.

I would love to research of publishing as an *experience*, as an exercise of public relation and compassion.

One question I have been asking myself recently is: <q>how to keep up with the world without being overwhelmed?</q>, and in particular: <q>how to stay informed and share critical insights without going crazy?</q>

I asked this to the few XPUB students connected during the open day, and it incentivized me to continue to pursue these questions.

## Retro-self retro-computing

Even though I can appreciate its meaning, I have never truly felt any passion for retrocomputing. This is odd, since I am a right-to-repair advocate, and I am all for [Perma-Hybridity](https://networkcultures.org/geert/2024/08/24/principles-of-perma-hybridity/). Still, it never got me as hard as, for example, the Fediverse does.

This was until I found the first smartphone I have ever owned, a 15-years-old-3-inch-relic, in a random drawer at my parents’. In that moment, something sparked in me.

What if I use retro-computing as a practice to recover and remember my retro-self? How about using *our own* old tools as devices of representation and publication of memories? For example, I am now trying to port the phone to [postmarketOS](https://postmarketos.org), then turn it into a static webserver, and finally publish from it my photos and writings created at the time I was using it.
