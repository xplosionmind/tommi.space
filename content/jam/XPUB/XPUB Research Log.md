<p class=date><time datetime='2026-09-21T07:58:08+02:00'>Monday 21 September 2026</time></p>

<cite>[Tepito already built the future](https://sala.red/en/cultura/tepito-ya-construyo-el-futuro)</cite> by [Violeta Ayala](https://www.violetaayala.com/ 'Violeta Ayala’s personal website') confirms the direction I want to pursue. Neighbourhoods don’t need anyone but themselves to start the change they want to see in the world.

> I spend my time in rooms where people debate who is going to build the future — with which models, with what capital, with whose permission — and then I land in a barrio that already built it without asking anyone.

This reminds me of what my friend [Giuseppe]() told me while showing me around [Scampia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scampia) the most undervalued neighbourhood of Napoli, with a troubled yet illuminating history of redemption: <q>We have the lowest voter turnout in Napoli, not because people do not believe in politics here, but because they believe they don’t need a politician to do something they can already do themselves</q> (the quote is originally in Italian and I am both translating and remembering it myself, so it is not super exact).

<p><time datetime='2026-09-20T23:34:21+02:00'>Sunday 20 September 2026</time></p>

Making the theme/intro song for [Barrio Bards Banquet](Barrio%20Bards%20Banquet.md) has made me work with Audacity for the first time in several years. It is a familiar and satisfying feeling to get my hands of a tool I used to use a lot, but with sleek and refreshed UX and UI.

I believe sound, voices, music is a powerful starting point to get back to talking to real people about what matters. The line between a cheesy and fanciful podcast and an entertaining and insightful radio show is very thin. I want to walk this line and be a bit trash but also to be thoughtful and deep, exactly as I am trying to make my intro sound like.
