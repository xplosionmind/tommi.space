## Initial idea

*Transcription of an audio recorded on <time datetime=2026-07-26T16:32:43+02:00>26<sup>th</sup> July 2026 at 16:32</time>.*

The neighbourhood as a way, as a space, and as a community to reclaim digital, rather than sovereignty, digital control, digital autonomy, digital awareness, and digital ownership (even though, fuck private property).

The power of communities of people that do not choose each other, the power of communities that spontaneously arise by people somehow ending up living in the same area, and proximity creating a community.

There are two contrasting images, unsurprisingly American and Western images that exacerbate this simple truth.

One example is Meta’s virtual reality project, the <q>Metaverse</q>, aimed at creating merely *virtual* proximities by absolutely overlooking, disregarding, brutally bypassing the concept of neighbourhood, simply because it’s not a way to extract value out of it. There is no way to extract digital capital and value out of actual proximity. Therefore, the failed experiment of the Metaverse is a statement to the inevitable power of the neighbourhood, and it is an assist to what real neighbourhoods can do.

A second antithesis is represented by <q>intentional communities</q>[^1]: people who are privileged enough to choose their community. Thus, they are intentional communities considering intentional factor in the people you choose to live with. Of course, this is not intrinsically bad per se, but it is quite an *artificial* community that arises out of previous relationships, networks of privileges, or experiences. How about empowering existing neighbourhoods in terms of digital technology?  

[^1]: E.g. the initiatives represented by the [Foundation for the Intentional Community](https://www.ic.org/about-us/), which I discovered when I visited <cite>[The Embassy](https://www.ic.org/directory/community/the-embassy/)</cite> in San Francisco. Like-minded people gather on communication channels such as the Slack <cite>[An Accidental Megastructure](https://the-megastructure.slack.com)</cite>.

The chief example is the existing practice of the repair cafe. A location, a beacon, an embassy, a headquarter, a reference point within the neighbourhood, meant to go beyond repair and digital autonomy. Offering concrete practical solutions to the people’s needs of repairing and fixing devices can be a way to channel this very concrete and petty need a more political angle and message: repairing instead of buying something new.

Secondly and most importantly, the repair cafe can improve the level of awareness of possibly using alternative tools, possibly escaping or limiting the power of Surveillance Capitalism.  

Nevertheless, the issue of repair cafés is their intrinsic nature: they emerge out of nerds living in a place and having a certain culture, sets of values, and experiences.

Why aren’t there more repair cafes? How can we go beyond the repair cafe and create repair cafes in areas where it’s absolutely not expected to have one?

Every context and city that I know has the <q lang=it>cinese</q> or the extra-communitarian who probably is underpaid and misrecognised, misrepresented, simply fixing phones or other devices. What if we go beyond this and we politicise the practice of fixing devices? We go deeper this subject, we go one step further: we know that the dispossession of capital, the digital extractivism and all the processes that enable surveillance capitalism are ultimately not located in the devices we own. Therefore, the core of repair cafes actually lies outside their concrete practice, but rather in servers, in external structures, in externalized computing power, externalized services, subcontracting the hell out of everything; delegating computing power, delegating everything to big tech.

What if the Repair Café can become an example of a neighbourhood server, providing neighbourhood services as a community centre does in the physical domain. Existing community centers can become something like this, to address related digital challenges in addition to their current practice.

As a result, rather than trusting an unknown, and actually with very bad reputation, big tech corporations with our data and our information, and especially accelerating and enabling the process of power imbalance and absolute insustainability of digital infrastructure we have today, what if we aim to distribute, re-decentralize servers and actual services, not as artistic experiments, not as experimental practices, but as actual services that might need…  
As a community might need, and actually serve the needs of a community, rather than creating even more friction.
